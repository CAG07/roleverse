# The One Ring (Second Edition) Reference

**Status:** Baseline reference content. This is an original, independently written
summary of core mechanics from The One Ring Roleplaying Game, Second Edition
(Free League Publishing). It's a fallback for the Rules Arbiter and Loremaster when a
campaign has no uploaded rulebook, and uploaded rulebooks take priority. The One Ring
has no open license or system reference document, so nothing here is copied from the
books: no rules text, tables, or setting prose.

## How the Rules Arbiter should use this document

- Ground answers in the sections below when a question matches one of these topics:
  attributes and Target Numbers, the Feat and Success dice, skills, Hope and Shadow,
  Weary and Miserable, Endurance and Load, combat stances and tasks, wounds and
  piercing blows, journeys, councils, the Fellowship phase, and advancement.
- If the campaign has an uploaded rulebook, prefer it over this summary.
- Exact numbers that change by culture or item (starting Endurance, armour values,
  weapon Injury ratings, journey event results) are not reproduced here. When a
  question needs one, say so and point the player to their core book.
- The Loremaster (the game's name for the GM) has final say, and the game expects
  them to adjust difficulty to the story.

## Core Resolution: Feat Die and Success Dice

A roll in The One Ring uses two kinds of dice:

- The Feat die is a twelve-sided die numbered 1 to 10, with two special faces.
  The Gandalf rune is the best result: it means automatic success. The Eye of
  Sauron is the worst result and counts as zero.
- Success dice are six-sided dice. A hero rolls as many as their rating in the
  skill being used. A 6 shows a special rune (the Tengwar rune) as well as its
  number.

Add the Feat die result to the Success dice total. If the total equals or beats the
Target Number (TN), the action succeeds. Each Tengwar rune rolled on a successful
roll improves the quality of the success: one gives a great success, and two or
more give an extraordinary success.

A skill rating of 0 still lets the hero roll the Feat die alone.

## Favoured and Ill-Favoured Rolls

Some situations make a roll favoured (roll two Feat dice and keep the better) or
ill-favoured (roll two Feat dice and keep the worse). A hero has favoured rolls with
their favoured skills. If a roll is both favoured and ill-favoured, the two cancel
out.

## Attributes and Target Numbers

Heroes have three attributes: Strength, Heart, and Wits. Each has a rating
(typically from 2 to 7 at creation). Each attribute sets a Target Number of 20 minus
the rating, so a higher rating means an easier TN:

- Strength TN for Strength skills and close-combat attacks.
- Heart TN for Heart skills.
- Wits TN for Wits skills.

The derived ratings are maximum Endurance (from Strength), maximum Hope (from Heart),
and Parry (from Wits). Each adds a value set by the hero's culture.

## Skills

There are 18 skills, six for each attribute:

- Strength: Awe, Athletics, Awareness, Hunting, Song, Craft.
- Heart: Enhearten, Travel, Insight, Healing, Courtesy, Battle.
- Wits: Persuade, Stealth, Scan, Explore, Riddle, Lore.

Each skill has a rating from 0 to 6. A hero's culture and calling mark some skills as
favoured, which makes their rolls favoured.

Combat proficiencies are rated separately: Axes, Bows, Spears, and Swords. Unarmed or
improvised fighting uses Brawling, which is rolled at a penalty based on the hero's
best proficiency.

## Valour and Wisdom

Valour measures courage. It is rolled to resist Shadow from Dread and some sorcery.
Raising Valour grants a Reward (an improvement to a weapon or armour).

Wisdom measures inner strength. It is rolled to resist Shadow from Greed. Raising
Wisdom grants a Virtue (a special ability, often tied to the hero's culture).

## Hope

Hope is the hero's reserve of determination. Spending 1 Hope on a roll adds one extra
Success die (two if the hero is Inspired, for example when acting on a Distinctive
Feature that fits the moment). Hope is also the threshold for the Miserable condition
(see below).

Heroes regain Hope by resting in safe places, during the Fellowship phase, from the
Fellowship pool, and from some Virtues and story events.

## Shadow and Shadow Scars

Shadow points measure the darkness creeping into a hero's spirit. Heroes gain Shadow
from four sources:

- Dread: terror, horror, and the presence of evil (resisted with Valour).
- Greed: the lure of treasure and power (resisted with Wisdom).
- Misdeeds: cruel or dishonourable actions. These can't be resisted.
- Sorcery: the dark magic of the Enemy (resisted with Valour or Wisdom, depending on
  its nature).

A successful Shadow test reduces or removes the gain. Each Tengwar rune can cancel an
extra point.

When a hero's Shadow points equal or exceed their current Hope, they become
Miserable. When Shadow reaches their maximum Hope, the hero suffers a bout of
madness. Afterwards their Shadow resets, but they gain a permanent Shadow Scar and
move a step along their calling's Shadow Path, which can give them a Flaw.

Shadow Scars can be healed during the Fellowship phase.

## Endurance, Load, and Weary

Endurance is the hero's physical stamina and ability to keep fighting. Damage,
exhaustion, and hardship reduce current Endurance.

Load is the total encumbrance of the gear the hero carries (weapons, armour, shield,
travelling gear) plus Fatigue gained on journeys.

When current Endurance is equal to or lower than Load, the hero is Weary. While
Weary, Success dice showing 1, 2, or 3 count as zero. The condition ends when
Endurance rises above Load again, usually through rest.

At 0 Endurance the hero drops and is knocked out.

## Miserable

When Shadow points equal or exceed current Hope, the hero is Miserable. While
Miserable, rolling the Eye of Sauron on the Feat die is an automatic failure, and on
some rolls it can trigger a bout of madness.

## Combat: Stances

At the start of each combat round, every hero chooses a stance, from most aggressive
to most cautious:

- Forward: fighting at the front. Attacks are easier, but the hero is also easier to
  hit.
- Open: a balanced stance with no adjustment.
- Defensive: protecting oneself. Attacks are harder, but the hero is harder to hit.
- Rearward: staying behind the line to use ranged weapons. A hero can stay rearward
  only while enough companions hold close-combat stances in front of them.

Heroes act in stance order, forward first. Enemies attack the heroes engaged with
them.

## Combat: Attacks and Damage

To attack, a hero rolls the Feat die plus Success dice equal to their combat
proficiency for the weapon. The TN is their Strength TN plus the opponent's Parry.

On a hit, the target loses Endurance equal to the weapon's Damage rating. Each
Tengwar rune on a successful attack can be spent on a special success, for example:

- Heavy Blow: the target loses extra Endurance.
- Fend Off: the attacker's Parry improves for the round.
- Pierce: improves the Feat die result, making a piercing blow more likely.
- Shield Thrust: with a shield, knock the enemy back so its next attack is weaker.

Some weapons have extra special successes of their own.

## Piercing Blows and Wounds

If an attack's Feat die result is high enough (10, or the Gandalf rune, for most
weapons), it's a piercing blow. The target must make a Protection roll, rolling
Success dice equal to their armour value against the weapon's Injury rating. On a
failure, the target is Wounded.

A Wounded hero keeps fighting but needs time to recover. A hero who is wounded again
while already Wounded, or is struck down another way, may be Dying. Wounds take days
to heal, and the Healing skill speeds that up.

Enemies follow similar rules. Many simply die when a piercing blow gets through.

## Combat Tasks

Each stance unlocks a combat task the hero can use instead of attacking:

- Forward: Intimidate Foe (Awe), to weaken the enemies' resolve.
- Open: Rally Comrades (Enhearten), to let companions recover Endurance.
- Defensive: Protect Companion (Athletics), to guard an ally from attacks.
- Rearward: Prepare Shot (Scan), to make the next ranged attack better.

## Adversaries

Enemies in The One Ring have:

- An Attribute Level, used for their rolls.
- Endurance.
- Might: how many wounds they can take.
- Hate or Resolve: a pool spent on fell abilities. Orcs and evil creatures use Hate;
  Men use Resolve.
- Parry and Armour.

Fell abilities are special powers fuelled by Hate or Resolve. When an adversary's
pool runs out, it is usually weakened, flees, or loses heart.

## Eye Awareness and the Hunt

During the Adventuring phase the Loremaster tracks Eye Awareness: how close the Enemy
is to noticing the company. It rises when heroes reveal themselves (using magical
items openly, rolling the Eye of Sauron at bad moments, fame). When it reaches the
Hunt Threshold, a Revelation episode happens: a twist where the Shadow strikes back.
Awareness then resets.

## Journeys

Long overland travel is played as a journey. Each hero takes one or more roles:

- Guide: leads the way and makes the main travel rolls.
- Hunter: finds food.
- Look-out: watches for danger.
- Scout: finds safe paths and campsites.

The journey is split into stages by distance and terrain. Each stage, the company
makes travel rolls and the Loremaster draws journey events. The Feat die result sets
how good or bad an event is, and the hero whose role fits the event rolls to deal
with it. Heroes collect Fatigue, which adds to Load until they rest. When they
arrive, the final event can set the mood of the arrival.

## Councils

Important conversations are played as a council. The Loremaster sets a Resistance
(how many successes are needed to convince the other side) and an attitude. The
heroes make an introduction (often a Courtesy or Awe roll) and then a set number of
interaction rolls with social skills. Tengwar runes add extra successes. If they
reach the Resistance in time, the council goes their way.

## The Fellowship Phase

Between adventures the heroes take a Fellowship phase, usually lasting weeks or
months. It's when they recover, grow, and pursue personal goals:

- Recover: regain Hope and Endurance, remove Fatigue, and heal wounds.
- Spend Skill Points to raise skills, and Adventure Points to raise combat
  proficiencies, Valour, and Wisdom.
- Choose an undertaking, for example: heal Shadow Scars, meet a patron, study magical
  items, raise an heir, write a song, or strengthen the fellowship.

A Yule Fellowship phase at the end of each year is longer and allows more
undertakings.

## Fellowship Points and Fellowship Focus

The company has a shared pool of Fellowship points, set by the number of heroes and
grown by the company's deeds. A hero can spend from the pool to regain Hope, with the
agreement of the group.

Each hero also picks a Fellowship Focus: another hero they're especially bound to.
When the focus is harmed (wounded, Miserable, or worse), the hero can gain Shadow.
When the focus does well, the hero can regain Hope.

## Callings, Cultures, and Standard of Living

The six callings are Captain, Champion, Messenger, Scholar, Treasure Hunter, and
Warden. Each gives favoured skills, a calling feature, and its own Shadow Path (the
kind of darkness that tempts it).

The core heroic cultures are Bardings, Dwarves of Durin's Folk, Elves of Lindon,
Hobbits of the Shire, Men of Bree, and Rangers of the North. Each gives a Cultural
Blessing, attribute options, derived-rating bonuses, starting skills, and a Standard
of Living.

Standard of Living (Poor, Frugal, Common, Prosperous, Rich, Very Rich) measures
everyday wealth. Treasure found on adventures can raise it during the Fellowship
phase.

## Rewards, Virtues, Useful Items, and Patrons

- Rewards: weapon and armour improvements, gained by raising Valour.
- Virtues: personal or cultural abilities, gained by raising Wisdom.
- Useful items: favoured travelling gear that makes certain rolls favoured.
- Patrons: powerful allies such as wizards and lords. Patrons give the company
  purpose and a fellowship bonus.

## Glossary

- Adventure Points (AP): earned in play, spent in the Fellowship phase on combat
  proficiencies, Valour, and Wisdom.
- Calling: a hero's vocation (Captain, Champion, Messenger, Scholar, Treasure Hunter,
  Warden).
- Council: a structured social scene with a Resistance to overcome.
- Distinctive Features: personality traits that can make a hero Inspired when they
  fit the moment.
- Eye Awareness / Hunt Threshold / Revelation: the Loremaster's tracker for how close
  the Enemy is to noticing the company, and the twist when it fills.
- Eye of Sauron ("the Eye"): the worst Feat die face, counted as zero.
- Fatigue: gained on journeys; adds to Load.
- Feat die: the d12 rolled on every test.
- Fellowship Focus: another hero your character is bound to.
- Fellowship phase / Yule: the downtime between adventures, and the longer one at the
  end of each year.
- Fellowship points: a shared pool the company can spend to regain Hope.
- Flaw: a lasting fault gained along a Shadow Path.
- Gandalf rune: the best Feat die face, an automatic success.
- Hate / Resolve: an adversary's pool for fell abilities.
- Loremaster (LM): the game master.
- Might: how many wounds an adversary can take.
- Miserable: Shadow equal to or above current Hope.
- Parry: added to the attacker's TN when someone attacks the hero.
- Piercing blow: a high Feat die roll on a hit that forces a Protection roll.
- Protection / Injury: the armour roll against a weapon's Injury rating.
- Skill Points (SP): earned in play, spent on skills in the Fellowship phase.
- Success dice: the d6s rolled equal to a skill rating.
- Tengwar rune: the 6 on a Success die; improves a success.
- TN: Target Number (20 minus the attribute rating).
- Undertaking: a Fellowship phase activity.
- Weary: current Endurance equal to or below Load.

## RoleVerse Character Sheet Fields

What each field on RoleVerse's The One Ring 2E character sheet holds:

- Age, Heroic Culture, Cultural Blessing, Standard of Living, Calling, Shadow Path,
  Patron, Distinctive Features, Flaws, History: the hero's identity and story.
- Rewards (Adventure Points, Skill Points, Fellowship Points): unspent advancement
  points and the hero's share of the Fellowship pool.
- Valour and Wisdom: the two ratings rolled for Shadow tests.
- Target Numbers (Strength, Heart, Wits): the TN for each attribute's skills (20
  minus the attribute rating).
- Endurance (Max, Current, Load, Fatigue): the hero is Weary when Current is equal to
  or below Load plus Fatigue.
- Hope (Max, Current, Shadow, Scars): the hero is Miserable when Shadow is equal to or
  above Current Hope. Scars are permanent Shadow Scars.
- Conditions: current states such as Weary, Miserable, or Wounded.
- Injury: details of a current wound and how long it takes to heal.
- Combat Proficiencies (Axes, Bows, Spears, Swords): Success dice for attacks with
  each weapon type.
- Skills: the 18 skill ratings.
- War Gear: one row per weapon with its Damage, Injury rating, Load, and notes.
- Armour & Helm: one row per armour piece with its Protection and Load.
- Shield: Protection, base and total Parry, and Load.
- Treasure: treasure carried, which can raise Standard of Living during the
  Fellowship phase.

## GM Quick Reference

- Difficulty: the TN comes from the hero's attribute, so the Loremaster mostly shapes
  difficulty by making rolls favoured or ill-favoured, by requiring more successes
  or Tengwar, and by consequences on failure, rather than by changing the number.
- When to roll: only roll when failure is interesting and the outcome is uncertain.
  Routine tasks for a skilled hero just succeed.
- Building a fight: the heroes act as a group, so match adversaries to the company.
  About one ordinary foe per hero is a fair fight. A leader, a large creature, or
  foes with deep Hate pools make it hard. Ambushes and bad ground make it much worse.
- Watch the resources: a fight's real cost is Endurance, Hope, and Shadow. Several
  small encounters on a journey can wear a company down more than one big battle.
- Shadow pacing: hand out Dread, Greed, and Sorcery gains at moments that fit the
  story, not on every scene. A Shadow test should feel like a turning point.
- Rewards: award a few Skill Points and Adventure Points each session, and more at
  the end of an adventure. Treasure matters through Standard of Living, not item
  shopping.

## Commonly Confused Rules

- The Gandalf rune is an automatic success, whatever the total. The Eye of Sauron
  counts as zero; it's only an automatic failure while the hero is Miserable.
- Tengwar runes only improve a roll that already succeeds.
- Weary turns Success dice showing 1, 2, or 3 into zeros. It doesn't stop the hero
  from acting.
- The attack TN is the attacker's Strength TN plus the target's Parry, not a fixed
  number.
- Endurance loss knocks a hero out at 0. Wounds come from failed Protection rolls
  after piercing blows, and they're tracked separately from Endurance.
- TOR 2E vs 1E: second edition uses three attributes (Strength, Heart, Wits) with TN
  = 20 minus the rating, reworked journeys into roles and events, added Eye Awareness,
  and replaced first edition's Specialties and Traits with Distinctive Features and
  Flaws. First edition material can be adapted, but the numbers don't carry over
  directly.
- The Rules Arbiter should treat "The One Ring" as second edition unless the player
  says otherwise. RoleVerse doesn't support first edition.
