# Fallout: The Roleplaying Game (2d20) Reference

**Status:** Baseline reference content. This is an original, independently written
summary of core mechanics from Fallout: The Roleplaying Game by Modiphius
Entertainment (2d20 system). It's a fallback for the Rules Arbiter and GM when a
campaign has no uploaded rulebook, and uploaded rulebooks take priority. Fallout is
a licensed property with no open rules text, so nothing here is copied from the
books: no rules text, tables of items or creatures, or setting prose. Players should
check their own book for exact values.

## How the Rules Arbiter should use this document

- Ground answers in the sections below when a question matches one of these topics:
  S.P.E.C.I.A.L. attributes, skills and tag skills, the 2d20 skill test, Action
  Points, Luck points, combat turns, attacks, Combat Dice and damage effects, hit
  locations, damage resistance, injuries, radiation, survival needs, perks, and
  advancement.
- If the campaign has an uploaded rulebook, prefer it over this summary.
- For specific weapons, armor, chems, creatures, or perk text, use training knowledge
  and say the answer isn't from an indexed source.
- The game master in Fallout 2d20 is called the gamemaster (GM). Their own pool of
  Action Points is a key balancing tool.

## Attributes: S.P.E.C.I.A.L.

Characters have seven attributes, typically rated 4 to 10 for humans (some origins
can go higher or lower in specific attributes):

- Strength: carrying, melee and unarmed damage.
- Perception: awareness, energy weapons, explosives.
- Endurance: toughness, hit points, big guns.
- Charisma: social interaction.
- Intelligence: science, medicine, repair.
- Agility: small guns, throwing, sneaking, defense.
- Luck: hit points and Luck points.

## Skills and Tag Skills

There are 17 skills: Athletics, Barter, Big Guns, Energy Weapons, Explosives,
Lockpick, Medicine, Melee Weapons, Pilot, Repair, Science, Small Guns, Sneak, Speech,
Survival, Throwing, and Unarmed. Skill ranks go from 0 to 6.

Each character picks Tag skills (usually three, plus one from their origin). Tag
skills start with extra ranks and widen the critical success range (see below).

## The Skill Test

A skill test uses an attribute and a skill chosen by the GM, for example Agility +
Sneak. The target number is the attribute plus the skill rank.

1. Roll 2d20.
2. Each die that rolls equal to or under the target number is one success.
3. A die that rolls a 1 is a critical success and counts as two successes. With a Tag
   skill, any die equal to or under the skill rank is a critical success.
4. A die that rolls a 20 is a complication: something goes wrong even if the test
   succeeds. Some situations widen the complication range (19-20, and so on).

The test's difficulty is the number of successes needed, from 0 (trivial) to 5
(nearly impossible). Difficulty 1 or 2 is common.

Extra successes beyond the difficulty become Action Points.

## Action Points (AP)

Action Points come from successes beyond what a test needs. They go into a party
pool, which holds up to 6, and can be spent by anyone in the group to:

- Buy extra d20s for a test, up to 5d20 in total. The first extra die costs 1 AP, the
  second 2, and the third 3.
- Obtain information: ask the GM a question about the situation.
- Reduce the time a task takes.
- Take an extra major action in combat (2 AP, once per turn).
- Add Combat Dice to an attack's damage (about 1 AP per extra die, with limits by
  weapon type).

The GM has their own AP pool (sometimes called Threat in other 2d20 games). It starts
each session with points and grows when players buy dice by paying into it, when
complications happen, and from some enemy actions. The GM spends it to make enemies
stronger, add reinforcements, or trigger hazards. At the end of a scene, unused
party AP drain away one at a time.

## Luck Points

Each character has Luck points equal to their Luck attribute. They refresh at the
start of each session. A Luck point can be spent to:

- Act first in combat, interrupting the normal turn order.
- Re-roll dice on a test.
- Use the Luck attribute in place of the usual attribute for a test.
- Find a useful item that could plausibly be there ("luck of the draw").
- Adjust a small detail of the scene, with the GM's agreement.

## Combat: Turns and Actions

Combat goes in rounds. Each character gets one turn per round, in order of
Initiative (Perception + Agility, with modifiers from perks, chems, and armor). On
their turn a character takes one minor action (move within the zone, draw a weapon,
take a chem, aim) and one major action (attack, sprint, defend, first aid, interact
with something complex). Extra actions can be bought with AP.

Distance is measured in range bands (Close, Medium, Long, Extreme) and zones.

Defense is how hard a character is to hit: usually 1, or 2 with high Agility. It sets
the difficulty for attacks against them.

## Attacks

An attack is a skill test using the weapon's skill and its matching attribute:

- Small Guns: Agility.
- Energy Weapons: Perception.
- Big Guns: Endurance.
- Explosives: Perception.
- Throwing: Agility.
- Melee Weapons and Unarmed: Strength.

The difficulty starts at the target's Defense (often 1 or 2). Ranged attacks get
harder outside the weapon's ideal range band. Aiming lets you re-roll a d20, and
cover, darkness, and other conditions adjust the difficulty.

## Combat Dice and Damage Effects

Damage is rolled with Combat Dice (CD): six-sided dice read like this:

- 1: 1 damage
- 2: 2 damage
- 3 or 4: 0 damage
- 5 or 6: 1 damage plus one Effect

Each weapon has a damage rating in CD (for example, 4 CD). Melee and unarmed attacks
add bonus CD from high Strength. Effects trigger once for each Effect rolled,
depending on the weapon's damage effects, for example:

- Burst: hit additional nearby targets.
- Breaking: reduce the target's cover or DR.
- Persistent: the target keeps taking damage on later turns.
- Piercing X: ignore X points of DR.
- Radioactive: add radiation damage.
- Spread: extra hits to other locations.
- Stun: the target loses its next turn's actions.
- Vicious: extra damage.

## Damage Types and Damage Resistance

There are four damage types: physical, energy, radiation, and poison. Armor and
creatures have Damage Resistance (DR) against each type, often per hit location. DR
is subtracted from damage of that type.

## Hit Locations

For a humanoid target, roll 1d20 for the location:

- 1-2: Head
- 3-8: Torso
- 9-11: Left Arm
- 12-14: Right Arm
- 15-17: Left Leg
- 18-20: Right Leg

Some creatures have their own location tables (insects, robots, mutated animals). An
attacker can try a called shot at a specific location for a higher difficulty.

## Hit Points and Injuries

Maximum hit points are based on Endurance plus Luck, and rise by 1 each level.

A critical hit happens when one hit deals enough damage after DR (5 or more), or when
damage brings a character to 0 hit points. A critical hit inflicts an Injury to the
struck location, for example a concussion to the head, or a crippled arm that makes
some tests harder.

At 0 hit points a character is dying. They must be stabilized with Medicine (or a
stimpak) before the end of their next turn, or they die. Injuries are treated with
Medicine, chems, doctors, or rest.

## Radiation

Radiation damage doesn't reduce current hit points directly. It reduces maximum hit
points instead, as rads build up. Rad-X raises radiation DR for a time, and RadAway
removes rads. Some origins, such as ghouls, heal from radiation instead of being
harmed by it.

## Survival: Food, Water, Sleep, and Fatigue

Characters need food, water, and sleep. Going without builds up Hunger, Thirst, and
Sleep deprivation, which add Fatigue. Each point of Fatigue makes tests harder (it
raises the difficulty of related tests or reduces hit points, depending on the rule
option in use). Eating, drinking, and sleeping remove it. Irradiated food and water
add rads.

## Chems and Addiction

Chems (Buffout, Jet, Med-X, Mentats, Psycho, and others) grant temporary bonuses.
Using an addictive chem calls for a test against its addiction rating. A character
who becomes addicted suffers penalties while not on the chem until the addiction is
cured.

## Carry Weight

Carry weight is set by Strength (in the core rules, a base amount plus a bonus per
Strength point). Going over it makes physical tests harder and can reduce movement.
Power armor and perks raise it.

## Power Armor

Power armor is worn piece by piece on a frame and powered by fusion cores. Each piece
has its own hit points and very high DR. Damage hits the armor piece before the
wearer. The frame raises Strength and carry weight while worn. Fusion cores drain
over time and with use.

## Perks and Advancement

Characters gain experience points for completing quests, overcoming encounters, and
good roleplaying. Each new level gives:

- +1 maximum hit points.
- A new Perk, chosen from those whose S.P.E.C.I.A.L. and level requirements they
  meet. Many perks have several ranks with rising level requirements.
- Skill ranks to raise skills (limited to rank 6).

## Origins

Characters choose an origin, which sets traits and starting gear:

- Brotherhood of Steel Initiate.
- Ghoul.
- Super Mutant.
- Mister Handy robot.
- Survivor.
- Vault Dweller.

Supplements add more, such as Minutemen and Children of Atom.

## Scavenging, Crafting, and Caps

Characters scavenge locations for junk, ammunition, food, and chems. The GM sets how
much a location has. Junk breaks down into common, uncommon, and rare materials,
used with perks and the Repair or Science skill to craft and modify weapons, armor,
chems, and robots at workbenches. Bottle caps are the common currency. Barter tests
improve prices.

## Glossary

- 2d20: the core roll, two twenty-sided dice against a target number.
- AP: Action Points, the party pool (up to 6) from extra successes. The GM has a
  separate pool.
- Caps: bottle caps, the common currency.
- CD: Combat Dice, the d6s used for damage (1 = 1, 2 = 2, 3-4 = 0, 5-6 = 1 plus an
  Effect).
- Complication: a 20 rolled on a test (or a widened range); something goes wrong.
- Critical success: a die rolling 1, or equal to or under the skill rank on a Tag
  skill; counts as two successes.
- Defense: the base difficulty of attacks against a character (usually 1 or 2).
- DR: Damage Resistance, by damage type and often by location.
- Effect: a weapon's special property triggered by a 5 or 6 on Combat Dice.
- Fatigue: from hunger, thirst, and lack of sleep; makes tests harder.
- Fusion core: power source for power armor and some weapons.
- Initiative: Perception + Agility, sets turn order.
- Luck points: equal to Luck, refresh each session.
- Major / minor action: one of each per turn.
- Origin: a character's background type (Vault Dweller, Ghoul, and so on).
- Perk: an ability gained at each level.
- Rads: accumulated radiation, reducing maximum hit points.
- Range bands: Close, Medium, Long, Extreme.
- S.P.E.C.I.A.L.: Strength, Perception, Endurance, Charisma, Intelligence, Agility,
  Luck.
- Tag skill: a specialty skill with extra ranks and a wider critical range.
- TN: target number (attribute + skill rank).
- Zone: an area of the battlefield used for movement and range.

## RoleVerse Character Sheet Fields

What each field on RoleVerse's Fallout 2d20 character sheet holds:

- Origin: the character's origin and its traits.
- Defense, Initiative: the difficulty of attacks against the character, and turn
  order (Perception + Agility, plus modifiers).
- Action Points: the character's share of, or note on, the party AP pool.
- Luck Points (Current, Max): Max equals the Luck attribute, and they refresh each
  session.
- Damage Resistance (Normal, Energy, Radiation, Poison): overall DR by damage type.
  Per-location armor DR goes in the Armor table.
- Radiation (Current Rads, Threshold): rads taken, which reduce maximum hit points.
- Skills: ranks in the 17 skills.
- Tag Skills: the tagged skills, with critical successes on rolls equal to or under
  the skill rank.
- Perks, Traits, Addiction: perks gained by level, origin traits, and any current
  addictions.
- Karma, Caps: moral standing (if the campaign uses it) and money.
- Weapons: one row per weapon with Damage (in CD), Range band, Rate of Fire, Ammo,
  and Qualities (damage effects and weapon properties).
- Armor: one row per location with its Damage Resistance and Weight.
- Faction Reputation: standing with each faction.
- Companion, Background: companions (robots, dogs, followers) and story.

## GM Quick Reference

- Difficulty: 0 needs no roll, 1 is routine for a trained character, 2 is
  challenging, 3 is hard, 4 is very hard, and 5 is nearly impossible. Most tests
  should be difficulty 1 or 2.
- Use your AP pool: start each session with a few points, add to it when players buy
  dice or roll complications, and spend it to give enemies extra actions, extra
  damage, reinforcements, or environmental trouble. That's how you scale a fight
  during play.
- Building a fight: compare the creatures' level and type with the party. Normal
  creatures are grunts that drop quickly, mighty creatures can stand up to a few
  characters, and legendary creatures are boss fights. A group of normal creatures
  around the party's level, plus one mighty creature, is a solid encounter.
- The wasteland is the threat: radiation, hunger, thirst, scarce ammunition, and
  broken gear wear characters down between fights. Use scavenging and survival to set
  the pace.
- Rewards: give experience for creatures overcome (each has an XP value), quests, and
  clever play. Loot (caps, ammunition, junk, chems) is the main material reward.

## Commonly Confused Rules

- A die equal to or under the target number is a success. A 1 is always a critical
  success; with a Tag skill, any die equal to or under the skill rank is too.
- A 20 is a complication even when the test succeeds.
- No test can use more than 5d20 in total, and the party AP pool holds at most 6.
- On Combat Dice, 3 and 4 deal no damage. Only 5 and 6 trigger Effects.
- Radiation lowers maximum hit points; it doesn't deal normal damage to current hit
  points.
- DR is checked by damage type. Armor with high physical DR may do little against
  energy weapons.
- Luck points refresh at the start of each session, not after rests.
- Fallout vs other 2d20 games (Conan, Star Trek Adventures, Dune): Fallout uses hit
  points, hit locations, and Combat Dice, and calls the GM's pool AP rather than
  Threat or Momentum. Rules from other 2d20 games don't transfer directly.
