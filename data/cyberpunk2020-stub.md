# Cyberpunk 2020 Reference

**Status:** Baseline reference content. This is an original, independently written
summary of core Cyberpunk 2020 mechanics (R. Talsorian Games). It's a fallback for
the Rules Arbiter and Referee when a campaign has no uploaded rulebook, and uploaded
rulebooks take priority. Cyberpunk 2020 has no open license, so nothing here is
copied from the rulebooks: no rules text, weapon lists, or setting prose. Where a
specific number is given, it's a widely known core value. Players should check their
own book for exact tables.

## How the Rules Arbiter should use this document

- Ground answers in the sections below when a question matches one of these topics:
  stats, derived values, roles and special abilities, skill checks, the Friday Night
  Firefight combat system (initiative, ranged and melee attacks, hit locations,
  armor, damage, wounds, stun and death saves), cyberware and Humanity, netrunning
  basics, reputation, and facedowns.
- If the campaign has an uploaded rulebook, prefer it over this summary.
- For specific weapon statistics, cyberware costs, or Netrunning programs, use
  training knowledge and say the answer isn't from an indexed source.
- Cyberpunk 2020 has several supplements (Chromebook, Solo of Fortune, Rache
  Bartmoss' Brainware Blowout). Rules there can override the core book. Flag that
  when it matters.

## Stats

Characters have nine stats, usually rated 2 to 10:

- INT (Intelligence): problem solving, knowledge.
- REF (Reflexes): agility, combat skill. The most important combat stat.
- TECH (Technical Ability): building and fixing things.
- COOL: calm under pressure, willpower, intimidation.
- ATTR (Attractiveness).
- LUCK: points spent to add to rolls. Each point used is gone until the next game
  session.
- MA (Movement Allowance): speed.
- BODY (Body Type): strength, endurance, toughness.
- EMP (Empathy): relating to people. Also sets Humanity.

Stats are generated with a pool of Character Points (rolled, or chosen by the Referee
for the campaign's power level) split between the nine stats.

## Derived Values

- Run: MA x 3 meters per combat turn. Leap: Run divided by 4.
- Lift and carry: BODY sets how much a character can carry (about BODY x 10 kg) and
  dead-lift (about BODY x 40 kg).
- Save Number: equal to BODY. Used for stun and death saves.
- Body Type Modifier (BTM): reduces all damage taken. Very Weak (BODY 2) 0; Weak
  (3-4) -1; Average (5-7) -2; Strong (8-9) -3; Very Strong (10) -4; Superhuman (11+)
  -5. Damage after BTM is never less than 1.
- Humanity: EMP x 10.

## Roles and Special Abilities

Each character has a role, and each role has a special ability that works like a
skill rated 1 to 10:

- Solo: Combat Sense (adds to initiative and Awareness).
- Rockerboy: Charismatic Leadership (sway crowds).
- Netrunner: Interface (operating in the Net).
- Techie: Jury Rig (temporary repairs).
- Media: Credibility (getting stories believed and broadcast).
- Cop: Authority (backed by the law).
- Corporate: Resources (calling on corporate assets).
- Fixer: Streetdeal (finding contacts and goods on the street).
- Nomad: Family (calling on the nomad pack).
- Medtechie: Medical Tech (advanced medicine and surgery).

Each role also has its own career skill package. The special ability and career
skills use career skill points (usually 40). Pickup skills, from points equal to
REF + INT, cover everything else.

## Skill Checks

To use a skill: Stat + Skill + 1d10, compared with a Difficulty number. Typical
difficulties:

- Easy: 10
- Average: 15
- Difficult: 20
- Very Difficult: 25
- Nearly Impossible: 30

In an opposed check, both sides roll and the higher total wins.

On a natural 10, roll again and add it (it can keep exploding). A natural 1 is a
fumble: the Referee may call for a roll on the fumble table for the skill type, and
the result ranges from nothing happening to a disaster.

Luck points can be added to any roll before it's made.

## Lifepath

Character backgrounds come from the Lifepath: cultural origin and language, personal
style, family background (family status, childhood environment, crisis),
motivations (personality traits, valued person, valued possession, feelings about
people), and life events for each year of adult life (big problems and wins,
friends, enemies, romance). It's a story tool more than a rules system.

## Friday Night Firefight: Initiative and Turns

A combat turn is about 3 seconds. Initiative is REF + 1d10 (Solos add Combat Sense).
Characters act from highest to lowest.

On their action, a character can usually move up to their Run and take one action,
such as one attack or one burst. Extra actions are possible at -3 to every action
that turn, for each extra action.

## Ranged Attacks

A ranged attack is REF + the weapon skill + the weapon's accuracy (WA) + 1d10,
against a difficulty set by range:

- Point Blank (touching, about 1 meter): 10
- Close (up to 1/4 of the weapon's range): 15
- Medium (up to 1/2 range): 20
- Long (up to full range): 25
- Extreme (up to twice range): 30

Common modifiers include aiming (+1 per turn spent aiming, up to +3), a moving
target, the target's size, firing two weapons, called shots at a specific location
(-4), and darkness or smoke.

## Burst, Full Auto, and Suppressive Fire

- Three-round burst: with a weapon that can burst, at close or medium range, get +3
  to hit. On a hit, 1d3 rounds hit.
- Full auto: with a high-rate-of-fire weapon, fire part or all of the magazine at one
  or more targets. At close range you get +1 per 10 rounds fired, and at long range
  -1 per 10 rounds. Each point the roll beats the difficulty by is one more round
  hitting, up to the number fired.
- Suppressive fire: spray an area to keep people down. Anyone moving through it must
  make an Athletics save, or take hits.

## Hit Location

Roll 1d10 for where a hit lands (or use a called shot):

- 1: Head
- 2-4: Torso
- 5: Right Arm
- 6: Left Arm
- 7-8: Right Leg
- 9-10: Left Leg

## Armor, Stopping Power, and Damage

Armor has a Stopping Power (SP) for each location it covers. To work out damage:

1. Roll the weapon's damage.
2. Subtract the SP of the armor at that location. If nothing is left, the armor
   stopped it.
3. Head hits double the remaining damage.
4. Subtract the target's Body Type Modifier. The result is never below 1.

Staged penetration: each time a hit gets through the armor, that location's SP drops
by 1. Armor-piercing ammunition treats armor as half its SP. Edged weapons and
monoblades work against soft armor in special ways.

Layering armor gives a partial bonus based on the difference between the two layers'
SP. It's not simply the two values added together. Heavy layers also lower REF
through encumbrance.

A limb that takes more than 8 points of damage from a single hit is severed or
destroyed. The victim must immediately make a death save as if Mortal 0. A single
head hit over 8 points means death for an unarmored target.

## Wounds, Stun, and Death

A character can take 40 points of damage, tracked in ten wound levels of 4 points
each:

- Light (points 1-4): no penalty.
- Serious (5-8): -2 REF.
- Critical (9-12): REF, INT, and COOL halved.
- Mortal 0 to Mortal 6 (13-40): REF, INT, and COOL reduced to one-third.

Stun saves: every time a character takes damage, they roll 1d10 and must get equal to
or less than their Save Number (BODY), minus a penalty for their current wound level
(0 at Light, -1 at Serious, -2 at Critical, -3 at Mortal 0, and so on). On a
failure, they're knocked out until they pass a stun save on a later turn.

Death saves: at any Mortal wound level, the character must make a death save every
turn: 1d10 equal to or less than BODY minus their Mortal level. On a failure, they
die. Stabilizing them (with First Aid or Medical Tech) stops the death saves.

## Melee and Martial Arts

Melee attacks are REF + Melee (or a Brawling or Martial Arts skill) + 1d10, with the
defender opposing with a dodge or parry roll. Melee damage depends on the weapon plus
a strength bonus from BODY. Martial arts styles add their skill level to some moves
(strikes, kicks, throws, holds) and grant special bonuses.

## Cyberware and Humanity

Cyberware (cybereyes, cyberarms, speedware, interface plugs) gives big advantages but
costs Humanity. Each piece has a Humanity Cost, usually rolled on dice when
installed.

- Every 10 points of Humanity lost lowers EMP by 1.
- At 0 EMP (Humanity 0 or less) the character becomes a cyberpsycho: a non-player
  character under the Referee's control.
- Therapy can recover some lost Humanity, at great expense.

Installing cyberware needs surgery, coded by severity: Negligible, Minor, Major, or
Critical. Each code sets the time, cost, and damage taken.

## Netrunning Basics

Netrunners jack into the Net through interface plugs and a cyberdeck. Their presence
in the Net is a program-driven icon. Key ideas:

- Interface (the Netrunner special ability) plus INT is the base for most Net
  actions.
- A cyberdeck has a speed, a data wall strength, and memory units (MU) to hold
  programs.
- Programs come in types: intrusion (breaking code gates and data walls),
  decryption, detection and alarm, anti-IC, anti-system, stealth, utilities, and
  anti-personnel.
- Data Fortresses protect corporate systems with data walls, code gates, and ICE
  (intrusion countermeasures).
- Black ICE attacks the runner directly and can damage or kill them in real life.

The Net is split into regions connected by long-distance links, and moving through
the Net takes time. The original Net rules are detailed and were heavily revised in
later supplements. Keep rulings simple unless the group uses a specific supplement.

## Reputation and Facedowns

Reputation (0 to 10) tracks how known a character is for their deeds, good or bad.

A facedown is a staredown before a fight. Each side rolls COOL + Reputation + 1d10.
The loser is unnerved: -3 on actions against the winner for the encounter, or they
back down.

## Gear, Money, and Lifestyle

Money is in eurodollars (eb). Characters earn a monthly income set by their role and
special ability level. Housing and lifestyle (from living on the street to corporate
luxury) are monthly costs. Weapons are rated for type, weapon accuracy,
concealability, availability, damage, shots, rate of fire, and reliability.

## Healing

Without medical care, a character recovers about 1 damage point per day after being
stabilized. First Aid and Medical Tech treatment speed this up (roughly doubling the
rate at higher skill levels). Speedheal drugs, nanosurgeons, and cyber-replacements
are other options, at a price.

## Glossary

- AP: armor-piercing ammunition (halves the target's SP).
- Avail: weapon availability (Excellent, Common, Poor, Rare).
- Black ICE: Net defenses that attack the runner's body and mind.
- BTM: Body Type Modifier, subtracted from damage after armor.
- Combat Sense: the Solo special ability; adds to initiative and Awareness.
- Conc: weapon concealability (Pocket, Jacket, Long coat, Not concealable).
- Cyberpsycho: a character whose Humanity has fallen to 0; becomes an NPC.
- Data Fortress: a protected computer system in the Net.
- Death save / stun save: 1d10 rolled under BODY minus a wound-level penalty.
- eb: eurodollars, the currency.
- Facedown: an opposed COOL + Reputation + 1d10 staredown.
- FNFF: Friday Night Firefight, the combat system.
- Fumble: a natural 1 on a skill check.
- ICE: intrusion countermeasures in the Net.
- IP: Improvement Points, spent to raise skills.
- Lifepath: the background generator.
- MA, BODY, EMP, REF, INT, TECH, COOL, ATTR, LUCK: the nine stats.
- MU: memory units in a cyberdeck.
- Rel: weapon reliability (Very Reliable, Standard, Unreliable).
- Rep: Reputation.
- ROF: rate of fire (shots per turn).
- Save: the Save Number, equal to BODY.
- SP: Stopping Power of armor at a location.
- Staged penetration: armor losing 1 SP each time it's penetrated.
- WA: weapon accuracy, added to attack rolls.

## RoleVerse Character Sheet Fields

What each field on RoleVerse's Cyberpunk 2020 character sheet holds:

- Real Name, Age, Height, Weight, Hair Color, Eye Color: identity details. Handle and
  role come from the sheet's name and class.
- Body Type: the BODY category (Very Weak through Superhuman), which sets BTM.
- Humanity (Current, Total): Total is EMP x 10. Current drops with cyberware. Every
  10 points lost lowers EMP by 1.
- Move (Run, Leap, Swim): movement per combat turn, derived from MA.
- Save: the Save Number for stun and death saves (equal to BODY).
- Initiative: the initiative bonus (REF, plus Combat Sense for Solos).
- Current Wounds, Wound Status: damage points taken (out of 40) and the matching
  wound level (Light, Serious, Critical, Mortal 0 to 6).
- Armor: one row per hit location with its SP and notes.
- Weapons: one row per weapon with Type, Concealability, Availability, Ammo, rate of
  fire (ROF), Damage, and Shots.
- Cyberware: installed cyberware.
- Skills: skill ratings, including the role's special ability.
- Lifestyle, Eurodollars: monthly housing and lifestyle, and cash on hand.
- Improvement Points: unspent IP for raising skills.
- Reputation: Rep, used in facedowns.
- Interface Rating, Netrunning Programs: for Netrunners, their Interface ability and
  the programs on their deck.
- Friends, Family, Enemies & Lovers, Background: Lifepath results and story.

## GM Quick Reference

- Difficulty numbers: Easy 10, Average 15, Difficult 20, Very Difficult 25, Nearly
  Impossible 30. A professional (stat 6 + skill 4) succeeds at Average tasks most of
  the time and at Difficult tasks about half the time.
- Lethality: any gun can kill. Armor, cover, and BTM matter more than hit points.
  Signal danger clearly, and give players ways to avoid a straight gunfight (deals,
  facedowns, stealth, netrunning).
- Building a fight: street punks have combat skills around 3 to 4 and light armor.
  Corporate security and cops have 5 to 6 with good armor. Elite Solos have 7 or
  more, cyberware, and heavy armor. Two or three trained opponents per character is
  already a serious fight.
- Initiative order matters: a fight is often decided in the first turn. Let ambushes
  and preparation shape who goes first.
- Rewards: give Improvement Points each session for skills that were used well and
  for good roleplaying, plus money from jobs. Track rent and lifestyle so the money
  matters.
- Cyberware and Humanity: roll Humanity Cost at installation and track it. Losing
  EMP should change how other characters react to the character.

## Commonly Confused Rules

- Armor SP is subtracted first, then head hits are doubled, then BTM is subtracted.
  The result is never below 1 if anything got past the armor.
- Layered armor doesn't simply add up. The bonus depends on the difference between
  the two layers' SP.
- Staged penetration lowers a location's SP by 1 each time a hit gets through it.
- Luck has to be committed before the roll, and spent points are gone until the next
  session.
- A natural 10 explodes (roll again and add). A natural 1 is a fumble.
- Stun saves get harder at each wound level. Death saves only start at Mortal 0.
- Cyberpunk 2020 vs Cyberpunk RED: RED (the newer edition) uses a hit point pool
  instead of the 40-point wound track, has no hit locations by default, uses
  Difficulty Values with different modifiers, ablates armor differently, and has its
  own Humanity and netrunning rules. Don't mix RED rules into a 2020 game unless the
  group chooses to.
